Where does the myth break down?

Below is the standard picture of the 1960s counterculture, followed by four claims. Mark every claim the lecture identifies as a reason the label oversimplifies. There are three.

The standard picture: the typical member of the counterculture was a young rock music fan who supported the civil rights movement and opposed the Vietnam War.

Which of the following does the lecture raise as a problem with that picture? Select all that apply.

Marked: 0

Why these three and not the others

The three qualifications all attack the same assumption — that opposing the war, loving rock, and holding progressive politics were one package carried by one group of people. Each shows the package coming apart: older opponents of the war who did not like the music, young fans of the music who were not political, and progressive movements that were not internally just.

The other two statements are true, and both appear in the lecture — but neither is a criticism of the counterculture label. Rock’s centrality and the reach of the draft are part of the explanation for why the phenomenon existed at all. Being true is not the same as being a qualification, and telling those apart is most of what historical reading is.
